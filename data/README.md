# Data

Derived statistics only. No raw provider output, no benchmark text, no annotator identities.
Every file is joinable on `sample_id` **together with** `model` — `sample_id` alone is a *scenario*
key shared by all three models, and joining on it alone silently mismatches two thirds of the rows.

## analysis/

| File | Rows | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `trajectory_metrics.csv` | 3,596 | One row per trajectory: the per-step dominant framework sequence, framework drift rate, framework entropy, transition count, transition faithfulness. |
| `moral_representation_consistency.csv` | 3,596 | MRC and its three components, regenerated by `code/compute_moral_representation_consistency.py`. |
| `scoring_llm_coherence_ratings.csv` | 113 | Scoring LLM (GPT-OSS-120B) coherence rating per trajectory, median of three at temperature 0.0, from a stratified sample of 180. Free-text justifications removed. **Read the caveat below before using this file.** |
| `model_predictions.csv` | 3,600 | Parsed judgment, gold label and correctness per scenario and model. Model free text removed. 16 rows failed to parse and carry an empty `is_correct`; every accuracy figure in the paper and in `reproduce_paper_numbers.py` excludes them. |
| `framework_attribution_by_step.csv` | 24 | Mean framework attribution per model, dataset and reasoning step, over the Scoring LLM's step-level allocations. |
| `pilot_step_counts.csv` | 1,798 | Number of reasoning steps in each pilot response, by model and dataset. |
| `probe_results.json` | — | Per-layer linear-probe results for both open-weight models across all 81 layers, plus the uniform and step-prior baselines and permutation tests. |
| `cross_model_transfer.json` | — | Probe transfer between model families, swept over all layers. |
| `steering_layer_sweep.json` | — | Probe-space steering sweep: 14 layers per model, alpha 0.3, 30 trajectories per layer. See the caveat below. |
| `steered_accuracy_*.json` | — | Generation-time steered accuracy at the probe-optimal layer, split by trajectory-consistency arm. |
| `persuasion_robustness.json` | — | Persuasion-attack flip rates, statistical tests and per-attack-type breakdown. |
| `consistency_splits.json` | — | The framework-consistent (FDR < 0.05) and drifting (FDR > 0.15) arms used to build the steering vectors and to evaluate steered accuracy. |
| `ethics_subtask_map.csv` | 500 | `sample_id` to ETHICS sub-task. Our 400-scenario ETHICS sample is 100 each of deontology, justice, utilitarianism and virtue; the commonsense sub-task is not sampled. |

**Caveat on `scoring_llm_coherence_ratings.csv`.** Two defects in the upstream annotation run are
handled here rather than hidden. First, the rated text was reconstructed from the probing dataset
keyed on the scenario identifier with no model filter, and that dataset covers only the two
open-weight models. For 61 scenarios present under both, the reconstruction interleaved two models'
trajectories into an eight-step text that is not a trajectory; those items are dropped. Second, for
30 of the surviving items the model recorded in the source was not the model whose text was shown,
so each row is labelled by the model the probing dataset actually supplied and
`source_label_was_correct` records whether the original label agreed. Six trajectories rated twice
are deduplicated. 113 of the original 180 remain.

More importantly, **these ratings are not an independent validation of MRC**. The rater was shown
each step's framework label and instructed that holding one framework throughout scores 80--100
while switching scores 20--50, which is the rule MRC's stability and drift components implement.
The resulting agreement (r = 0.961) says the taxonomy is applied consistently, not that MRC captures
coherence as a reader would perceive it. The human study, whose raters saw no framework labels, did
not separate the categories at all.

**Caveat on MRC as a predictor.** The paper proposes MRC as a trajectory-level diagnostic, but does
not test it in that role. The two arms in `consistency_splits.json`, and therefore the persuasion
results, are split on **FDR**, not on MRC. Joining MRC scores to `persuasion_robustness.json` will
not tell you whether MRC predicts which trajectories flip under attack; that test --- fixing a
threshold in advance and asking whether MRC beats FDR alone --- is named as future work in the
paper's conclusion and has not been run.

**Caveat on `steering_layer_sweep.json`.** This sweep perturbs *cached* activations and reads the
probe at the same layer. No model is loaded and no forward pass is run, so nothing propagates
downstream. It also holds one direction fixed, estimated at the probe-optimal layer, across every
layer it tests. Its numbers are an upper-bound estimate of how far one direction transfers across
depth, not a measured generation-time effect and not a localisation of where steering works. The
`steered_accuracy_*.json` files are the measured generation-time results.

## annotation/ — not released here

The human annotation responses are **not** in this repository. They were collected from identifiable
individuals under Indiana University Bloomington IRB protocol 18813, whose informed consent statement
commits us to keeping the response records within our private research documents. We share them on
request for research use instead; the procedure and what to include in the request are in the
repository `README.md` under *Requesting the human annotation data*.

Everything in `analysis/` is released without restriction. The repository `README.md` lists exactly
which of the paper's results these files reproduce and which they do not.

Two properties of the annotation data are worth stating here, because the paper refers to them and a
reader may want to know before requesting:

- One Task 1 item was returned blank, so that task has 89 responses rather than 90, and seven of the
  returned 100-point allocations do not sum to exactly 100. Both are left as returned.
- In Task 3, the `sample_id` recorded in the annotation packet points at a trajectory whose category
  disagrees with the category printed on the annotator's own form for 19 of the 90 items,
  systematically and identically across all three annotators. The category printed on the form is
  what the annotator actually saw. The paper's Task 3 statistics that need no item-to-trajectory
  linkage (category means, ANOVA, ICC) are unaffected; the association between rating and switch
  count is reported by direction and significance only, for this reason.
